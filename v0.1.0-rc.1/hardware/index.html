Hardware¶

The PF1550's package, pins, bus settings and power, as the driver uses them. The driver reaches the chip through the I2C bus and two optional pins, STANDBY and INTB; everything else on the package is wired by the board, as the datasheet's example schematic shows (section 13.1, p. 133).

Datasheet sections this page relies on

From NXP Semiconductors PF1550 7:

  • Pins and package: 4.2 Pin definitions, p. 8
  • I2C address, clock and register framing: 9.7 Control interface I2C block description, p. 71
  • Example schematic: 13.1 Example schematic, p. 133
  • Package outline: 14 Package outline, p. 139

All of them, with the driver API for each: Datasheet map.

Package¶

HVQFN-40, 5.0 mm x 5.0 mm with an exposed pad (data sheet section 14, p. 139). The exposed pad is ground. The body size is the only dimension the datasheet text gives; the pitch and the height are on its drawing and are not drawn here.

The driver uses SDA and SCL through the port, STANDBY through GpioSet(CtrlPin::Standby, ...) and INTB through GpioGet(CtrlPin::Intb, ...). It never touches the other pins: PWRON, ONKEY, WDI and RESETBMCU belong to the board and the state machine. The driver reads their effect (state, interrupts) and configures their debounce and polarity where the datasheet makes them registers.

Blocks¶

The PF1550's function blocks inside its outline. Arrows between blocks carry signals or data in the direction they point (both ways where they have two heads). The package pins stand on the left and right edges with their numbers, each wired to the block it reaches.

Drawn from: docs/hardware.yml; inc/pf1550_i2c_interface.hpp.

The register map is split in two pages by address: the PMIC registers 0x00 to 0x6F and the charger registers 0x80 to 0x9F, both on the same I2C address.

Control pins¶

  • STANDBY is an input of the chip. RequestStandby(true) calls your GpioSet(CtrlPin::Standby, GpioSignal::ACTIVE); ACTIVE means "request STANDBY" and your port maps the polarity (STANDBYINV = 0 is active high). The driver never writes STANDBYINV unless PowerConfig::apply is set: change the polarity only together with the port. Without the hook RequestStandby() returns Unsupported.
  • INTB is an open-drain output, low when an unmasked interrupt is pending. With GpioGet(), ServiceInterrupts() returns an empty result without bus traffic while INTB is high. Without it, every call reads INT_CATEGORY.
  • PWRON, ONKEY, WDI, RESETBMCU are not driver pins. PWRON level or edge sensitivity is an OTP option (OTP_PWRON_CFG, option A8 is edge sensitive) and the driver cannot read it.

An address that nothing acknowledges (no chip, no power, a wrong address) is reported as Error::NoDevice when your port has the optional GetLastFailure() hook; without it the driver cannot tell it from another bus fault and returns Bus.

Power¶

The PF1550 powers itself from VSYS and VBUSIN; the driver has no supply of its own to manage. The limits the driver needs from you describe what the cell and the loads tolerate (Configuration).

Silicon and options¶

ReadVariant() returns DEVICE_ID, OTP_FLAVOR, SILICON_REV and the address code. The ten orderable options A0 to A9 differ in their OTP start-up values (Table 85): output voltages, the power-up order, the charger's start-up mode, the input current limit and whether DVS is enabled. The driver reports the option and the sequencer helpers model the power-up order of A1 to A9 (API guide).

  • A0 parts are "not programmed, internal validation only" (Table 1 footnote). The driver supports reading them and assumes nothing about their start-up values.
  • SILICON_REV is stored raw and no decision is made on it: the datasheet's text and its register bitmap disagree on the reset value (ambiguity C22).
  • The OTP image itself is not readable over I2C in the public datasheet, so the driver cannot read the fuses back; it asks you for the two OTP facts it needs (BuckConfig::dvs, the declared DVS mode) and reports the rest from the option byte. See Datasheet map.
